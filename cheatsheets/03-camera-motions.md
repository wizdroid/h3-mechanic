# 03 — Camera motions

H3 only understands camera motion through words. Use the **official verbs**. Synonyms such as `drone shot`, `moves left`, `slow zoom effect` or `cinematic pan, 4k` are **not** moves.

## How to write a move

One natural sentence inside the shot, in this order: **official verb → modifiers only if they matter → what the move lands on**.

```
The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward the folded letter in her hands.
The camera pans right with large amplitude at fast speed, revealing the open doorway.
The camera holds a static shot as the runner exits the frame.
```

- Modifiers: `with small amplitude` / `with large amplitude`, `at slow speed` / `at fast speed`. Leave out medium amplitude and normal speed.
- Never put modifiers on a static shot, POV or shake.
- One move per shot. Two directions in one shot cancel each other; a second move is a new `[Shot N]`.
- Write the move as an action, not a tag list at the end of the sentence.
- The style sentence carries look and light only. It must not add a second move (no "handheld feel" in the style line when the shot is a dolly).

## Official moves

| Move | Exact H3 phrase | What physically happens | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static | `The camera holds a static shot` | Nothing moves | Dialogue, confessions, guilt, tension, any line that must be heard clearly |
| Push in (dolly in) | `The camera pushes in` | Body moves forward, parallax | Building emotion on a face, realisation, threat approaching |
| Pull out (dolly out) | `The camera pulls out` | Body moves back | Isolation, ending beats, revealing context. Never pull a speaking face into a wide |
| Zoom in / out | `The camera zooms in` / `The camera zooms out` | Lens changes, body still, no parallax | Period looks (80s crash zoom), surveillance feel |
| Pan left / right | `The camera pans left` / `The camera pans right` | Lens pivots horizontally | Following a glance, revealing a second person or a door |
| Tilt up / down | `The camera tilts up` / `The camera tilts down` | Lens pivots vertically | Revealing height (a prison wall, a towering officer), feet-to-face reveals |
| Truck left / right | `The camera trucks left` / `The camera trucks right` | Body slides sideways | Side-on travel, passing bars or windows, parallax reveals |
| Pedestal up / down | `The camera pedestals up` / `The camera pedestals down` | Whole camera rises / drops | Rising over a wall or crowd, settling to a seated character's eye line |
| Arc (orbit) | `The camera arcs around <Subject 1>` | Moves on a curve around the subject | Romance, heroic beats, objects. Use small amplitude on faces |
| Tracking | `The camera tracks <Subject 1>` | Follows a subject who is already moving | Walks, escorts, chases |
| POV | `The camera holds <Subject 1>'s point of view` | That subject's eyes | Peeking through bars, a keyhole, a frightened look. That subject's face is not visible |
| Shake slightly | `The camera shakes slightly` | Small handheld tremor | Handheld documentary feel, nervous moments |
| Shake strongly | `The camera shakes strongly` | Violent shake | Impacts, explosions, fights, earthquakes |
| Roll | `The camera rolls clockwise` / `The camera rolls counterclockwise` | Rotation around the lens axis | Disorientation, drunkenness, dream states |

| Locked-off establishing | `The camera holds a static shot on the prison gate at dawn` | Official |

## Picking the move from the beat

| Beat | Move | Framing note |
|---|---|---|
| A line that must be heard | Static, or slow push-in with small amplitude | Speaker MCU, front-facing |
| Confession, guilt, realisation | Slow push-in | CU or MCU |
| Order / command | Static, low angle on the speaker | MS or MCU |
| Shock reaction | Crash zoom (period) or fast push-in with small amplitude (modern) | Land on CU |
| Walk and talk | Tracking from the front | MS, steady distance |
| Escort / leaving / exit | Tracking from behind | Wide is allowed: no readable faces |
| Entrance through a door | Static from the far side of the room, or pan reveal | Let them arrive at MS |
| Reveal of a place | Pedestal up, pan, or pull out | Establishing wide, faces not readable |
| Romance, attraction | Arc with small amplitude at slow speed, or slow push-in | MCU two-shot, three-quarter faces |
| Chase | Tracking from behind (wide) then cut to tracking front (medium) | Slow the wide; speed lives in the subject, not the camera |
| Fight, impact | Shake strongly, short shots | MS; avoid wide faces |
| Nervous, unstable | Shake slightly | MCU |
| Peeking, surveillance | POV | The POV owner's face is not shown |
| Ending beat | Pull out at slow speed from a back view, or static | Pull out only when faces are turned away |

## Movement and face safety

- A face shown from the front or the side stays **CU, MCU or MS** for the whole move. Do not pull out, zoom out or pedestal up from a readable face into a wider framing.
- Fast speed plus wide framing melts faces. In wides keep the camera at slow speed and let the subject provide the motion.
- Do not let a person walk past the lens at close range during a move. Have them stop "in a clear medium framing".
